# Research report, submission package and assessment

Source: *BD_Individual_Assignments_2026_2027.pdf*, pages 8–9.

## Mandatory report structure (same for every assignment)

| Section | What it must explain |
|---|---|
| Title and short abstract | The question, method, main result and scope of the conclusion |
| Introduction and objective | The motivation, a focused research question and any hypothesis |
| Background and references | Relevant algorithms or techniques and sources that support the explanation |
| Methodology | Implementations, inputs, environment, correctness checks, measurement protocol and budgets |
| Results | Clearly labelled tables and plots, units, repetitions and variability |
| Discussion | An answer to the question, plausible explanations supported by evidence, and limitations |
| Conclusions | What the study establishes within its tested scope and what remains unresolved |
| References and materials | Full citations and the location or description of reproducibility materials |

Write the report **in English**. Keep it **concise and sufficiently detailed to reproduce the experiment**.
It is a course research report; a novel algorithm or publication-level contribution is not required.

## Submission contents
- A **PDF report** with your **name, assignment number and research title**.
- **Source code, a README**, dependency and environment details, and **exact build and run instructions**.
- **Raw results**, input generation or acquisition instructions, and **scripts that reproduce the analysis
  and figures**. Include reused materials or identify their exact version.
- Suggested names: `Assignment01_Surname_Report.pdf`, `Assignment01_Surname_Materials.zip`.
  **A repository such as GitHub is mandatory.**

## Figures, citations and oral explanation
- Label axes and units, name configurations, and explain what each figure shows.
- Cite borrowed ideas, code, text and figures. Use **one consistent reference style (IEEE)** and
  **verify that each source exists and supports the claim**.
- Possible oral presentation: explain the research question, the design that makes the comparison fair,
  the most informative results and the limitations. Be ready to explain the code and reproduce or
  interpret a selected experiment.

## Assessment criteria

| Criterion | Evidence of a satisfactory study |
|---|---|
| Correctness | Validated results and clearly documented numerical assumptions |
| Experimental design | Relevant question, fair baselines, controlled inputs and a justified protocol |
| Analysis | Interpretation of time, memory and scaling with variability and limitations |
| Reproducibility | Executable code, configuration details, raw data and analysis scripts |
| Communication | Clear English report, readable figures, accurate references and defensible conclusions |

## Generative AI policy (course)
AI tools may support explanation, planning, debugging or language revision. **Students must disclose
the tool, purpose and affected parts of the work.** They remain responsible for understanding the code,
validating measurements, verifying references and defending the conclusions. **Generated content is not
evidence. Fabricated results or references are unacceptable.**

→ Every report MUST contain a short "Use of Generative AI" statement (see the `scientific-report` skill).
